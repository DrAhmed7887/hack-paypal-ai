# BillCheck demo film: three storylines

All three use only real BillCheck screens (captured from the running app), real numbers from bill C08 ($4,445.00 billed → $1,882.38 suggested), the real PayPal sandbox checkout, and PayPal's own read-back (capture COMPLETED, invoice MARKED_AS_PAID). Nothing drawn claims to be the product.

## A. One bill, one number (recommended: a judge follows one real bill and sees every dollar earn its place)

- **Device / form:** the worked example where a number lands · object-to-object chain (each shot ends on the thing that opens the next).
- **What you see and feel:** the $4,445.00 total on a real bill becomes the thing the eye follows. BillCheck pulls the bill apart line by line; each flagged amount physically shrinks to its reference while its reason types in beside it, and the survivors stack into $1,882.38. That figure flies into the PayPal button, the payment lands, and the same lines print back as the invoice stamped paid.
- **First two seconds:** the real bill text fills the frame, the TOTAL CHARGES line "$4,445.00" pulses once, and the voice says "Four thousand, four hundred forty-five dollars."
- **Beats:** hook (the total) → tension (five lines, no prices to compare) → reveal (BillCheck named as the bill is dropped in) → proof (read · price · explain · refuse to guess) → payoff ($1,882.38 assembles, flies into PayPal, captured) → claim (one line) → end card (invoice paid, BillCheck mark, repo).
- **Look:** the app's own off-white ground and teal accent; real UI pieces lifted out at 1.6–2× with their real labels; the money figures in a tabular display face; the red only on the two flagged amounts.
- **Sound:** a calm, warm ElevenLabs narrator (your account), a soft pulse bed, a small tick each time a number changes and a register "cha-chunk" on the capture.

## B. The envelope

- **Device / form:** a moment in someone's day · a journey through three worlds (paper → phone → paper).
- **What you see and feel:** a hospital envelope on a kitchen table at night; the bill slides out and its paper becomes the BillCheck screen on a phone. The audit plays in the real UI; after the PayPal payment the corrected itemized invoice slides back out of the phone as paper, stamped paid, onto the same table.
- **First two seconds:** an envelope marked "Riverside Community Hospital (Synthetic)" opens; the bill's total "$4,445.00" is the brightest thing in the frame.
- **Beats:** hook (the envelope) → tension (the reader's eye scans five codes it can't price) → reveal (paper becomes the app) → proof (read · price · explain) → payoff (pay in PayPal) → claim → end card (paper invoice, paid).
- **Look:** a warm table-lamp world for the paper scenes (photographic paper texture, no invented logos), the app's clean ground inside the phone.
- **Sound:** room tone and paper sounds in the table world, a gentle piano bed, the ElevenLabs narrator.
- **Risk:** the table world is drawn, not captured; it must look like a scene, not a mockup, so it costs more build time.

## C. The math out loud (the bold one)

- **Device / form:** type carries the argument · cut on the beat.
- **What you see and feel:** giant kinetic numbers on colour fields, cut to a driving beat: "$2,400" → "Medicare: $426.30" → "× 2.79" → "$1,189.38". Each equation is followed by a half-second of the real BillCheck screen showing the same figure, as proof. The payoff is the PayPal capture counting up to $1,882.38 full-frame.
- **First two seconds:** a full-frame "$4,445.00" on red slams in on the first beat, then is struck through.
- **Beats:** hook (the total struck) → tension (the codes as a wall) → reveal (BillCheck) → proof (three equations, each proven on screen) → payoff (capture counter) → claim → end card.
- **Look:** two flat colour fields (the app's teal and off-white), one heavy display face for numbers, the real UI only in short proof inserts.
- **Sound:** a licensed or free beat-led track, hits on every cut, narrator sparse (only the claim lines).
- **Risk:** strong for attention, weaker on "show the project running", which Devpost asks for.

## Shared assumptions

- Length 1:45–2:15 (Devpost cap: under 3:00), 1920×1080, 30 fps.
- Voice: ElevenLabs on your Creator plan (about 1,500 characters of the 106,000 left this cycle; no extra cost).
- Music: a free track with a licence that allows it (checked before use); sound effects on visible actions.
- No third-party marks beyond PayPal's own checkout; card-brand logos stay blurred.
